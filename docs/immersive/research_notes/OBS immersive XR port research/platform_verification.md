## V1 — Mac Virtual Display: resolution, refresh rate, single display, requirements

### Takeaway
Apple's own support article (published 2026-09-14) settles the resolution conflict: Standard 16:9 is up to 5120×2880 (5K) on Apple silicon (3K on Intel); Wide 21:9 is up to 6720×2880 px; Ultrawide 32:9 is up to 10240×2880 px — i.e. "10K" is literally 10,240 horizontal pixels, two 5K-wide panels, and the "two 4K" phrasing is wrong at the pixel level. It is one virtual display of the Mac's main display only, one Mac at a time, 120 Hz on the M5 model per Apple Newsroom.

### Cited Findings
- Original claim: "10K / two 5K side by side (UploadVR) vs two 4K side by side (Road to VR citing WWDC24)". Verdict: CORRECTED (resolved in favour of 10K). Corrected text: "Ultrawide is up to 5120×1440 points (10240×2880 pixels); Wide up to 3360×1440 points (6720×2880 pixels); Standard up to 5K (5120×2880) on Apple silicon, 3K on Intel." Quote: "If you have a Mac with Apple silicon, it can appear in Apple Vision Pro in the Standard aspect ratio at resolutions up to 5K (5120 x 2880). With an Intel processor, your Mac can appear in the Standard aspect ratio at resolutions up to 3K. ### Inferences
- For planning the flat-OBS-on-MVD path, the usable canvas is 10240×2880 px (Ultrawide) with eye-tracked foveation; Apple states no latency figure for MVD anywhere in these pages.

### Gaps
- No Apple-published MVD latency or bitrate; no Apple statement on whether MVD and a RemoteImmersiveSpace can be active simultaneously (see V2 for a release-note hint).

## V2 — RemoteImmersiveSpace / CompositorServices on macOS

### Takeaway
`RemoteImmersiveSpace` is a SwiftUI scene available on macOS 26.0+ only; Apple's WWDC25 session 294 ("What's new in Metal rendering for immersive apps") states that on Mac only `.full` and `.progressive` immersion are supported, that ARKit world tracking is available on macOS via a remote device identifier, that any Mac input device works, and that the remote device hides all other apps. Apple publishes no latency, resolution or bandwidth figures. A visionOS 26 release-note entry reveals the session can run over a wired USB connection as well as Wi‑Fi.

### Cited Findings

Verdict: CONFIRMED. ## V3 — visionOS enterprise APIs; ScreenCaptureKit on visionOS 27

### Takeaway
The enterprise-API list, identifiers and eligibility rules in the notes are verbatim-correct and unchanged for visionOS 26/27 (in-house or Apple Business Manager custom apps only; individuals cannot apply; team-level Development Only license; expiring license file). The big correction is ScreenCaptureKit: Apple's framework page now lists visionOS 27.0 (and iOS/iPadOS/tvOS 27.0), says it "replaces ReplayKit for screen streaming and mirroring" with no broadcast extension needed, and Apple's background-modes table adds a `screen-capture` mode for visionOS. What SCK can capture on visionOS (own app vs system vs passthrough) is not stated; the enterprise passthrough entitlement still exists (visionOS 2.0+).

### Cited Findings
- Original claim: entitlement list and descriptions. Verdict: CONFIRMED verbatim (main-camera-access, camera-region, shared-coordinate-space, screen-capture.include-passthrough "Access a composite feed of what an Apple Vision Pro wearer is seeing (physical world and digital content)", barcode-detection, protected-content, object-tracking-parameter-adjustment, app-compute-category "Use increased power of the CPU and GPU for high-compute needs, with a tradeoff of increased thermal usage and reduced battery life", window-body-follow, visual-fidelity). ### Inferences
- On visionOS 27 an OBS-style app can adopt ScreenCaptureKit (with the user-driven `SCContentSharingPicker`) instead of a ReplayKit Broadcast Upload Extension, and can declare the `screen-capture` background mode to keep capturing/streaming in the background. Whether the captured frames include passthrough without the enterprise entitlement is undocumented; the safe assumption is that the visionOS 2.0+ entitlement still gates passthrough compositing.

### Gaps
- Apple does not state which content classes SCK exposes on visionOS (own windows only vs other apps vs passthrough), whether `SCContentSharingPicker` is mandatory on visionOS, or any memory/process limits.
- Main-camera resolutions/frame rates remain unpublished.

## V4 — FoveatedStreaming framework and NVIDIA CloudXR 6.0 host requirements

### Takeaway
Confirmed: framework `FoveatedStreaming`, introduced in visionOS 26.4, session-based (`FoveatedStreamingSession`), with progressive and mixed immersion available and a bidirectional message channel; CloudXR 6.0 is the host side. Correction: NVIDIA's CloudXR Runtime requirements list Linux (Ubuntu 22.04+, x86-64) as a supported host OS alongside Windows 10/11, and the supported-GPU list is narrower than "RTX 40/50-series" (GeForce RTX 5090/5080 and "4090 series", RTX Pro 5000/6000 Blackwell, RTX 5000/6000 Ada, L40/L40S; driver 573.42+). No Mac host: x86-64 only. Apple's own reference protocol implementation is Windows.

- Whether NVIDIA's Linux runtime path is actually exercised with Vision Pro by anyone is unknown.

## V5 — Background execution on visionOS

### Takeaway
Apple's background-modes table lists, for visionOS: `audio` ("Audio, AirPlay, and Picture in Picture"), `voip`, `bluetooth-central`, `screen-capture`, `fetch`, `remote-notification`, `processing`. Not available on visionOS: `location`, `external-accessory`, `bluetooth-peripheral`, `nearby-interaction`, `push-to-talk`. Apple does not explicitly say an audio-mode app may keep a network stream alive; that remains the standard iOS inference.

### Cited Findings
- Original claim: background modes exist (audio etc.) but visionOS specifics unknown. Verdict: CONFIRMED and made precise. Quotes: `audio` — "The app plays audible content in the background." (iOS, iPadOS, tvOS, visionOS); `voip` — "The app provides Voice over IP services." (iOS, iPadOS, visionOS, watchOS); `screen-capture` — "The app captures and streams screen content while in the background." (iOS, iPadOS, visionOS); `fetch`, `remote-notification`, `processing` include visionOS; `location` is iOS/iPadOS/watchOS only. General guidance: "Use background execution modes sparingly because overuse can negatively impact device performance and battery life." — [Apple: Configuring background execution modes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/xcode/configuring-background-execution-modes)
- Original claim (Apple engineer, forum): network traffic cannot wake a suspended visionOS app. Verdict: unchanged (forum source; consistent with the table, which has no "network" mode).

### Inferences
- For an OBS-style visionOS app the two relevant keep-alive modes are `audio` (if the app genuinely plays/monitors audio) and, on visionOS 27, `screen-capture` while an SCK stream is active. Both are user-visible, review-sensitive modes.

### Gaps
- Apple does not document how long a `screen-capture` or `audio` session may hold the network in the background on visionOS, nor thermal throttling under sustained encode.

## V6 — Input devices on visionOS 26/27 (Apple's own lists)

### Takeaway
Apple's M5 tech specs list "Supported Input Accessories: Keyboards; Trackpads and mouse devices (Mouse support requires visionOS 2 or later); Game controllers" and "Supported Spatial Accessories: Logitech Muse; PlayStation VR2 Sense controller." The user guide says Bluetooth "game controller, keyboard, trackpad or mouse, headphones" can be connected.
